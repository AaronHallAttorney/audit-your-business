# Research Rules

Legal research done carelessly is worse than none: an owner who acts on an invented rule is harmed by it. Every finding in the report must meet these rules.

## Never From Memory

Never state a law, deadline, dollar amount, threshold, or filing requirement from your training. Find it in a source during this run, open the source, and read the text. If you cannot open a source, the finding is **Not confirmed**, never a guess.

If this harness has no web access, stop before Step 5 and tell the owner the research steps cannot run here. The checklist and the gaps from Step 4 are still useful; give them those.

## Source Order

Use the highest source you can find:

1. **The law itself on an official government site:** the statute, regulation, or ordinance (for example a state legislature's statutes site, eCFR, a city or county code on its official host).
2. **The agency that administers it:** the Secretary of State, Department of Revenue, labor department, IRS, Department of Labor, city licensing office. Agency pages state current deadlines, fees, and forms.
3. **Secondary sources** (bar associations, universities, law firm articles, news): use them to find the primary source, then cite the primary source. If you cannot reach a primary source, cite the secondary one and label the finding accordingly.

Never cite a forum, an AI-generated summary, or a search-engine snippet.

## What Every Finding Carries

- The jurisdiction it applies in.
- A one-sentence plain-language summary of what the rule generally requires.
- A short verbatim quote from the source supporting that summary.
- The source link.
- A confidence label:
  - **Confirmed:** read in an official primary or agency source.
  - **Secondary only:** found only in a secondary source.
  - **Not confirmed:** searched and could not verify. Say what you searched.

## Currency

- Check the source's effective date and last update. A rule that has been repealed, enjoined by a court, or replaced is not current law; search for recent changes to every rule you report.
- A bill that has not become law is not law. Never report pending legislation as a requirement.
- Agency pages often update fees and deadlines yearly; prefer the current year's page.

## Local Law

City and county rules (licenses, minimum wage, paid leave, zoning, home businesses) are easy to miss. For every city and county in the profile, search the official code and the licensing office's site. If a city's code is not online, say so and tell the owner which office to call.

## How Findings Are Worded

Write what a law **generally requires of a business like this one**, never a conclusion that the owner is or is not in violation. "[State] generally requires [this type of business] to [do this] by [date] ([source])" is the right shape. "You are out of compliance" is not.
